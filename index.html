Understanding cap rate

Cap rate, in plain English: the year's profit before the mortgage, divided by the price — how hard each dollar of price works for you.

How to calculate it: (1) A year of rent — monthly rent × 12. (2) Subtract the year's costs — 5% vacancy, 5% maintenance, taxes and insurance — what's left is the NOI. (3) Divide the NOI by the price. Example, 1420 Derry St on the board: $17,400 rent − $870 vacancy − $870 maintenance − $2,900 taxes − $1,100 insurance = $11,660 NOI → $11,660 ÷ $139,900 = 8.3%.

  • Low scenario — below ~5%: low risk, low income. Nicer, steadier areas. Rent money is thin; the payoff leans on the home's value going up. The board's Low scenario listing is 2615 N Front Street, at 4.8%.
  • Medium scenario — ~6–8%: balanced. Solid rent for the price without the rough edges. The board's Medium scenario listing is 1420 Derry Street, at 8.3%.
  • High scenario — ~9% and up: higher risk, higher income. The big number usually comes with a catch: tougher block, older house, more repairs and tenant turnover. The board's High scenario listing is 1432 S 13th Street, at 10.6%.

Rule of thumb: a high cap rate is the market paying you extra to take on extra headaches.
