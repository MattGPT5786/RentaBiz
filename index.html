📐 How the numbers work

  • Loan: 20% down, 30-year fixed at 7.0% — principal & interest shown per month.
  • NOI: annual rent minus 5% vacancy, 5% maintenance, property taxes and insurance.
  • Cap rate: NOI ÷ price. Cash flow: NOI ÷ 12 minus the mortgage payment.
  • Calculating cap rate, step by step: (1) A year of rent — monthly rent × 12. (2) Subtract the year's costs — 5% vacancy, 5% maintenance, taxes and insurance — what's left is the NOI. (3) Divide the NOI by the price. Example, 1420 Derry St below: $17,400 rent − $870 vacancy − $870 maintenance − $2,900 taxes − $1,100 insurance = $11,660 NOI → $11,660 ÷ $139,900 = 8.3%.
  • Rent-to-price: monthly rent ÷ price (the "1% rule" yardstick).
  • Estimates only — verify taxes, insurance and rent before any offer.

➕ Adding a deal

Find a Harrisburg rental worth a look? Send the listing link (or the address, price, beds/baths and expected rent) and it lands on the board with the numbers run. Statuses keep track of where each deal stands between the two of you — Watching, Analyzing, Toured, Passed, or Offer.

Cap rate, in plain English: the year's profit before the mortgage, divided by the price — how hard each dollar of price works for you.

  • Below ~5% — low risk, low income. Nicer, steadier areas. Rent money is thin; the payoff leans on the home's value going up.
  • ~6–8% — the middle ground. Solid rent for the price without the rough edges. Two of the board's samples sit right here (7.7% and 8.3%).
  • ~9% and up — higher risk, higher income. The big number usually comes with a catch: tougher block, older house, more repairs and tenant turnover.

Rule of thumb: a high cap rate is the market paying you extra to take on extra headaches.
